THE IDEA

The conference will bring people to Mumbai. We want the hours around it to become part of the reason they remember the trip.

THE FORMAT

Three experiences across the week, with one simple purpose: helping visitors experience Mumbai in a way that feels local, warm and easy to join.

Why we are doing this
People will fly to Mumbai for Devcon. We want them to leave knowing a little more about India.

The week will bring together people from many countries and many first visits to India. The best parts of a trip are often the parts that were not on the conference schedule, so OnGround is building experiences that feel natural, social and worth remembering.

WHAT PEOPLE GET

They get a morning they can join easily, a taste of Mumbai during the day and an evening that shows them a side of India they cannot get inside a conference hall.

WHAT A PARTNER GETS

A chance to make one of those moments possible, with useful branding, real guest interaction and a story people will naturally photograph and share.

One week, three experiences

Different moments, one reason to come outside.

The three formats work together without feeling like one large event. Each one has a clear purpose, a clear audience moment and a clear place for a partner to contribute.

The easiest way to remember a city is to taste it.

During Devcon week, OnGround will bring small Mumbai food experiences to approved locations around the areas where visitors are staying and spending time. A cart can become the place where someone tries their first vada pav, first masala chai or first jalebi in Mumbai.

A partner can own the cart, the product and the moment.
WHERE

03 / India Night

An Indian celebration for people who came from everywhere.

India Night will bring together the food, music, colours and small rituals that make an Indian celebration feel unmistakably Indian, while keeping the evening comfortable for an international audience that may be experiencing all of it for the first time.

01

Guests will arrive to an Indian welcome with flowers, colour and small details that feel warm rather than staged.

02

The evening will move through Indian food, mehendi, lights, music and dhol before a short baraat moment brings everyone together.

03

The room will be built for talking, eating and dancing, so the cultural experience never becomes a performance that people watch from a distance.

04

Diwali will shape the feeling through light, colour and celebration without turning the evening into a costume version of India.

A modern Indian celebration with the parts people actually want to experience, eat, photograph and remember.
